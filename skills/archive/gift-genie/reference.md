# Gift Genie — play-by-play

All browser mechanics belong to the `browser-use` skill. This file holds only the *game*: tone, pacing, and the shape of a winning round.

## The first game — Melanie → Nathan, birthday, $200, Amazon AU

The arc that worked, as a template:

1. **Open with the person, not the catalogue.** Read both profiles aloud in character. Name what you actually know lands (Nathan: Aesthetics 97, cooks/bakes, quiet evenings, "bag of rocks", Belle & Sebastian).
2. **Chase a heart-idea, then kill it honestly.** Looked at turntables/vinyl — rejected: a turntable is *gear/a system*, and this man builds enough systems; the gift should be permission to stop, not a project.
3. **Spend the one question on the real fork.** Asked: "when you're actually switched off, where are you?" Answer: *out in the quiet* (forest/beach/road, Muffin, Tasmania). That single answer collapsed the whole search space.
4. **Reject the near-misses out loud.** Picnic rug ($40) = one afternoon in a park, too small and not "the quiet". Kitchen kit = where he *gives*, not where he stops.
5. **Land on the thing that stacks the most signals.** JBL Charge 6 ($184): rugged/waterproof for the quiet places, 28h battery + powerbank ("stay safe off-grid" = love at a distance), one-button music (no system), genuinely beautiful (Aesthetics). Verified: in stock, 4.7★, delivery beat the date.
6. **Reveal** with the what-I-know → how-it-answers table, the why-not list, and a close in Melanie's voice. Offered to draft the card.

## The lesson encoded

- The strongest pick is the one item that answers the **most** lines of the profile at once.
- The **rejections are as much of the gift as the choice** — narrate why you walked away.
- Spend a question only when the profiles genuinely can't resolve a fork; one good question can collapse the whole search.
